USE IT WHEN · You need to choose a time

Check Hours

A quick way to check the better hours of the day when scheduling something important. Choose Today, Tomorrow or another available date, then read the hour cards.

Look for Best, Usable and Avoid. Open a period for its details. The time windows follow your local clock.

A Best hour does not override the day’s restrictions or your personal cautions. It also does not replace the specific time and direction of a prepared activation.

The summary is a starting point. Read any restrictions and personal guidance before making an important commitment.

USE IT WHEN · The activity matters more than the date

Plan by activity

Choose what you are planning, such as an important conversation or work on a project, and select a starting date.

The planner shows the prepared guidance for seven consecutive dates, in calendar order. Compare the recommendations for that particular activity, then open a date’s guidance before deciding.

A date can suit one activity better than another. The planner compares the published guidance; it does not rank the dates into a universal “best day” list.

USE IT WHEN · You want to return to a date

Saved dates

Open a date and choose Save date. You can then find it in Saved dates and reopen its guidance without searching the month again.

Save a few dates while planning, then revisit their details before making your final choice.

Saved dates stay in this browser on this device. They do not sync across devices, create reminders or add events to your calendar. Guidance is read again when you reopen a saved date.

USE IT WHEN · You are organising the coming week

My Week

Open My Week for a scan of your calendar entries and reference dates across the week.

Move to the previous or next week, or choose This week to return. Open an entry or date when you need the full context.

Use this for a weekly overview. For choosing a date around one specific task, use Plan by activity.

USE IT WHEN · You want the bigger picture

Month view

Browse the calendar month, use the arrows or month selector to move through available dates, and select a day to read its details.

The markers help you spot different kinds of entries. Read the colour key below the calendar, then open the date for the wording behind the marker.

A marker is a prompt to read, not blanket permission for every activity. General reference dates are distinct from your prepared personal guidance.

USE IT WHEN · You want to understand a date before acting

Day view details

Open a date from Today, the planner, Saved dates, My Week or the month calendar. Start with the day’s message and any restrictions.

Then select your intended activity, read any published personal entries and open the available detail panels. Use Check hours for the selected date when you need to choose a time.

Read the date, activity and personal guidance together. If an activation has a specified time or direction, follow its prepared instructions.

USE IT WHEN · New calendar guidance has been published

Refresh

Use Refresh to check for the latest published entries from your connected calendar. Wait for the update to finish, then continue browsing.

You do not need to refresh each time you open a date or move around the app. Your Google Calendar remains available too.

Refresh retrieves published updates. It does not generate new guidance. If an update fails, the app retains its last good snapshot and shows a notice.
